PAGE 02 / COUNTERFACTUAL

What would Chennai look like without a modern IT industry?

Chennai would still be an important industrial city. But removing software, IT services and digital operations would change where graduates work, how office corridors function and how efficiently other sectors operate.

Thought experiment: this is not a prediction that IT will disappear. It is a way to understand how deeply technology is now connected to the wider Chennai economy.

01 / IMMEDIATE EFFECT

The city continues. The economic mix changes.

01 / TALENT

Graduate pathways narrow

Fewer software, IT-services, support and digital-product roles would push more engineering talent toward manufacturing, finance, healthcare, government and core engineering.

What replaces them?

Embedded systems, automotive engineering, industrial automation, electronics, medical technology, logistics and other technical services would take a larger share.

02 / OFFICE CITY

OMR and Taramani change character

Technology campuses and related commercial demand would shrink sharply.

Repurposing becomes necessary

Office districts could shift toward engineering R&D, corporate centres, education, health, laboratories and mixed-use development.

03 / SERVICES

Secondary demand falls

Transport, food, rentals, coworking, recruitment, facility management and business travel all depend partly on office employment.

The effect spreads

Removing IT changes more than the number of programmers; it changes the demand around them.

04 / INDUSTRY

Other sectors still need software

Cars, factories, hospitals, banks and ports depend on information systems and software.

So IT moves inside them

The realistic alternative to a standalone IT sector is not “no technology”; it is technology becoming embedded inside industrial companies.

02 / THE REAL LESSON

IT is no longer one industry sitting beside the others.

AUTO

Vehicles are software systems

EV controls, infotainment, connected services, ADAS, battery analytics and security all require software.

HEALTH

Hospitals run on digital infrastructure

Clinical systems, diagnostics, imaging, medical devices and patient operations increasingly depend on technology.

PORTS

Logistics is a data problem

Tracking, scheduling, customs, warehousing and optimisation require reliable digital systems.

THE CONCLUSION

Chennai without IT could still manufacture, heal, move goods and build products. But it would do all of those things less digitally.


The more realistic 2031 question is not “IT or no IT?” It is: where will technology create the most value inside Chennai’s other industries?
